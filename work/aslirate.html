AsliRate

A live web app that ranks supplier quotes by what each order really costs in rupees, not by the price printed on the quote.

StatusLive at aslirate.in
ContextAI in Business course, Masters' Union
My roleBuilt and launched it on its own domain
Open aslirate.in ↗

The problem

Small Indian businesses usually compare suppliers on the price per unit. That number leaves out most of the real cost: GST that can or cannot be claimed back as input tax credit, freight that one quote includes and another ignores, and minimum orders that force you to buy stock you do not need.

What it does

AsliRate takes two to six quotes in whatever form they arrive (a PDF, a WhatsApp screenshot, an Excel rate list or pasted text), reads them, and ranks the suppliers by landed cost. It adds freight and extras, prices in the extra stock a minimum order forces, and flags quotes that have expired or will miss the delivery date.

The approach

The main design decision was to split the work in two. AI reads the messy quotes; a fixed calculator produces every rupee figure, and the working is shown line by line so a buyer can check it or pass it to their CA.

Features

  • Reads PDFs, WhatsApp screenshots, Excel rate lists and pasted text
  • Ranks 2 to 6 suppliers by real landed cost
  • Counts GST credit, freight and minimum order quantities
  • Flags expired quotes and missed delivery dates

A worked example

In the worked example on the site, a buyer needs 5,000 shipping boxes. The supplier quoting ₹17.20 a box ends up at ₹36.20 once its 10,000-box minimum, freight and GST are counted, while a ₹19.50 quote works out to ₹16.53, the cheapest real cost of the three.

What I learned

Coming from audit, I knew a number is only useful if someone can trace how it was reached. AsliRate is the product version of that idea.
